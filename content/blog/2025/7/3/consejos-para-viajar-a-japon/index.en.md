This summer we went on vacation to Japan! I took some notes with travel tips and things that stood out to me, for friends who might want to visit. I am sharing them here too.

**Part 1 · Travel tips**

**1. When to go**

- May, June, September and October: mild weather and little rain. July to August = sauna; winter = cold + short days.

**2. How to go from Spain**

- Cheap and comfortable route: Madrid/BCN → Abu Dhabi → Tokyo (7 h + 2 h layover + 10 h) with Etihad. Iberia has direct flights that are not much more expensive, but only on 2 or 3 days each week. A return ticket costs around €600 to €1,000, depending on the dates.

**3. What cities to see**

The ideal trip is 15 days. The essential basic route takes 7 to 8 days. Tokyo (2 to 3 days) + Kyoto (1 to 2 days) + Nara (1 day) + Osaka (1 day). We also went to Miyajima, which was very cool, near Hiroshima (1 to 2 days), and Takayama + Shirakawa-go (2 to 3 days in the Japanese Alps).

**4. Cost and payments**

- The weak yen makes eating out cheap, around €10 to €15, and hotels for €80 to €150 are a bargain for Europeans.
- They now accept physical cards almost everywhere (Revolut works well). Eight years ago when I went for the first time it was not like this!
- Install Welcome Suica on iPhone → pay for metro, buses and kombinis (convenience stores) without friction.

**5. Internal transport**

- The Rail Pass is no longer worth it unless your itinerary is a marathon.
- Shinkansen (Japanese bullet train): €30 to €60 for a 400 km journey. It is extremely punctual and has had no passenger deaths caused by train accidents since 1964. Buy tickets from a machine 2 to 3 days before if you want a reserved seat, or on the same day. You will almost always get one and, in the worst case, you will have to stand. The website is hell, do not even try it. It is worse than Renfe!
- Uber works in Tokyo (at least) and it is not very expensive.

**5. Connectivity**

- Be careful with Hong Kong eSIMs: WhatsApp and ChatGPT are sometimes restricted; use a VPN or Nomad.
- A translation app is essential because few Japanese people speak English, including young people.

**6. Accommodation & experiences**

- Try a ryokan to live the tatami + onsen experience in rural towns and cities.
- Book Teamlab Borderless / Ghibli Park in advance if you intend to visit them.
- Copy free tour itineraries and adapt them with ChatGPT.

**7. Food**

- Have konbini onigiri and bananas for breakfast. They are cheap, tasty and easy to carry.
- Restaurant lists: follow and ask [@zordor](https://x.com/zordor) or [@luisete](https://x.com/luisete).

**8. Traveling with children**

- It is a very safe country, with comfortable trains and metros for traveling with children. There are also many attractions for them. For Spaniards, it is probably the easiest exotic country to visit with children.

Part 2 · Things that caught my attention about Japan 🗾🔍

**1. Technology and energy** ⚡

- You will barely see electric cars (neither Teslas nor Chinese brands) and few solar panels on the roofs, for the amount of sun they have (compared to Spain/Europe, they must not subsidize them as much as here)
- Toilets with a little jet and digital control even in modest places. In 15 days I did not see a single analog toilet, it is as if they were banned or something. I imagine there are some and they will be much cheaper but it is so embedded in the local culture that not even the shabby town bar considers not putting one like that.
- Japan's great technological pride is still the Shinkansen. Since 1964 it has had no passenger deaths caused by train accidents, and it runs with atomic clock punctuality. Trains are so frequent that it feels like a long distance commuter service passing through each city every 10 minutes. It is incredible, seriously. The whole world should aspire to a railway system at that level. We are close in technology but far behind in how we operate the system.

**2. Ready-made food** 🏪

- Konbini stores are open 24 hours and offer a thousand snacks. There is one on every corner, with almost everything and fresh, good quality food. 7 Eleven was invented in the USA, but the Japanese franchises did so well that they ended up absorbing the half bankrupt American parent company! Many Japanese people never cook and always buy ready made food.

Since there are so many konbini stores, delivery apps like Glovo do not seem to be popular. They may exist, but you do not see the city full of delivery riders on bikes with giant backpacks.

**3. Urban mobility** 🚌

- Almost no cars are parked on the street because spaces are limited and rules are strictly enforced. The streets look better. However, as in the UK or USA, the sidewalks are made of asphalt.
- There are many traffic guides with flags in places where other countries would use a traffic light. It is a way to give retirees work.

**4. Health and beauty** 👵

- There are few gyms compared with Spain and hardly any muscular Japanese people. The cult of muscle does not appeal to them. A more feminine kind of beauty is in fashion, as in South Korea.
- You will see many older women hunched over, apparently they have more osteoporosis than normal due to a lack of dairy, little calcium no matter how much fish they eat.

**5. Relationship with China** 🇨🇳

- I do not think I saw a single Chinese car or mobile phone, despite the 0% tariff. Consumers themselves prefer domestic products and distrust the giant next door. There is still so much historical hatred that it shows in things like this.

**6. Architecture** 🏯

- You will not see buildings touching each other. They are all separated to dissipate seismic waves.

Although Tokyo has almost 40 million inhabitants, almost everyone lives in small houses with 1 or 2 floors. Earthquakes favor this, creating an endless urban expanse whose edge you cannot even see from the Skytree.

**7. Time customs** 🕑

- Although the sun rises at 5 a.m. in summer, when they do not have blinds, and sets around 7 p.m., Japan does not use daylight saving time. The clocks never move forwards or backwards.
