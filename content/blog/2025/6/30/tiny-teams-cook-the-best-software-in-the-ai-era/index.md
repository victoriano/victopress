Demand for software that automates today’s inefficient, manual workflows is set to skyrocket: the faster and cheaper these systems become, the more deeply they’ll weave into everyday operations. Enter the Jevons Paradox: AI-driven efficiency fuels even greater appetite for technology, so the real challenge isn’t rationing tools but forming tiny, 10×-talent teams that can turn them into value through rapid iterations and deep in-house expertise.

- **Workflow & Task Automation**
  - The next wave will connect systems end-to-end and wipe out redundant manual work.
  - Tools that erase friction and redundancy see the fastest adoption and greatest impact.
- **Jevons Paradox meets AI**
  - Higher efficiency drives greater consumption of software and expert services.
  - Competitive edge comes from orchestrating the right tools, not reinventing them all.
- **Tiny teams hitting $100 M in 1-2 years**
  - A handful of specialists, amplified by AI, can scale product and revenue without middle layers.
  - Small size forces ruthless prioritization: zero useless meetings, processes on autopilot.
- **Speed ≠ more heads (Brooks’ Law & the pizza rule)**
  - Every new hire multiplies communication paths; teams of ≤8 often outperform 20.
  - One senior engineer + AI can match several mid-levels; keep cycles short and customer feedback constant.
- **10× / 1000× talent**
  - Exceptional contributors explore and ship where many stall—shield them from bureaucracy.
  - Don’t “promote” them to managers; their multiplication happens in code and design, not in spreadsheets.
- **In-house knowledge as an edge**
  - Innovation sparks when tacit and explicit know-how spin inside a tight internal loop.
  - Examples: Mercadona Tech (\<20 people) and Freepik (6 on AI) out-innovate consulting armies
